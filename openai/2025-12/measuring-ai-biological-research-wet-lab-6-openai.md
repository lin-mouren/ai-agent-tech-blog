# Measuring AI's capability to accelerate biological research in the wet lab

**December 16, 2025** | OpenAI

GPT-5 created novel wet lab protocol improvements, optimizing the efficiency of a molecular cloning protocol by 79x.

## Overview

Accelerating scientific progress is one of the most valuable ways AI can benefit humanity. OpenAI, working with Red Queen Bio (a biosecurity start-up), built an evaluation framework that tests how a model proposes, analyzes, and iterates on ideas in the wet lab.

The study set up a simple molecular biology experimental system and had GPT-5 optimize a molecular cloning protocol for efficiency.

## Key Finding: 79x Efficiency Improvement

Over multiple rounds of experimentation, GPT-5 introduced a novel mechanism that improved cloning efficiency by **79x**. This is compared to:
- Human researchers' 2.3-fold improvement
- An intermediate model's 2.13-fold improvement (8% of human performance)

Cloning is a fundamental molecular biology tool. The efficiency of cloning methods is critical for creating large, complex libraries central to protein engineering, genetic screens, and organismal strain engineering.

## Evaluation Framework

The evaluation framework tests how a model:
1. Proposes experimental approaches
2. Analyzes results
3. Iterates on ideas

This offers a glimpse of how AI could work side-by-side with biologists to speed up research, helping human researchers:
- Move faster
- Reduce costs
- Translate discoveries into real-world impact

## Biosecurity Implications

These evaluation results show that models can reason in and improve protocols, which may have implications for biosecurity. OpenAI is committed to:
- Building necessary and nuanced safeguards at model and product levels
- Developing evaluations to track current capability levels
- Partnering with government entities including US CAISI and UK AISI
- Working with Los Alamos National Lab to study AI's role in wet lab settings

This work is part of OpenAI's broader Preparedness Framework for evaluating and managing biological capability risks.
