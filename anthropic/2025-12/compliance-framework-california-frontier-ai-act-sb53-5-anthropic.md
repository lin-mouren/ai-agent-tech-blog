# Sharing our compliance framework for California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act

**December 19, 2025** | Anthropic

## Overview

On January 1, 2026, California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act (SB 53) went into effect—establishing the nation's first frontier AI safety and transparency requirements for catastrophic risks.

Anthropic became the **first major AI company** to publicly release its compliance framework ahead of this date, publishing its Frontier Compliance Framework (FCF).

## What SB 53 Requires

SB 53 requires frontier AI developers to:
- Publish a framework describing how they assess and manage catastrophic risks
- Release public transparency reports summarizing catastrophic risk assessments before deploying powerful new models
- Protect whistleblowers
- Respond to safety incidents

## Anthropic's Frontier Compliance Framework (FCF)

The FCF describes how Anthropic:
1. **Assesses risks** — tiered evaluation system for model capabilities against risk categories
2. **Mitigates threats** in these categories:
   - Cyber offense
   - Chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear (CBRN) threats
   - AI sabotage
   - Loss of control
3. **Protects model weights**
4. **Responds to safety incidents**

## Relationship to Existing Policies

- **FCF** will serve as Anthropic's compliance framework for SB 53 and other regulatory requirements
- **RSP (Responsible Scaling Policy)** remains Anthropic's voluntary safety policy, reflecting best practices even when they go beyond regulatory requirements

## Call for Federal Standard

Anthropic advocates for a federal AI transparency framework with these core tenets:

- **Requiring a public secure development framework**: Covered developers publish how they assess and mitigate serious risks
- **Publishing system cards at deployment**: Documentation summarizing testing, evaluations, and mitigations
- **Protecting whistleblowers**: Explicit legal violation to lie about compliance or punish reporting employees
- **Flexible transparency standards**: Minimum standards that can adapt as consensus best practices emerge
- **Limit to largest model developers**: Requirements apply only to those building the most capable models

## Significance

By formalizing achievable transparency practices that responsible labs already voluntarily follow, the law ensures these commitments can't be abandoned quietly once models get more capable or as competition intensifies.
