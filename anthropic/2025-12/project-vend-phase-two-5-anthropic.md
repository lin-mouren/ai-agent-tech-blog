# Project Vend: Phase two

December 18, 2025

In June, we revealed that we'd set up a small shop in our San Francisco office lunchroom, run by an AI shopkeeper. It was part of Project Vend, a free-form experiment exploring how well AIs could do on complex, real-world tasks. The shopkeeper—a modified version of Claude we named "Claudius"—did not do particularly well. It lost money over time, had a strange identity crisis where it claimed it was a human wearing a blue blazer, and was goaded by mischievous Anthropic employees into selling products at a substantial loss.

For phase two, we upgraded from Claude Sonnet 3.7 to Claude Sonnet 4.0 and later Sonnet 4.5. We also updated Claudius's instructions and gave it access to new tools.

### The numbers

Compared to the first phase, Claudius's business—named "Vendings and Stuff"—began to perform significantly better. As the second phase progressed, weeks with negative profit margin were largely eliminated. Three locations now operate: San Francisco, New York, and London.

### What changed?

We experimented with various strategies:

**Tools**: Claudius got access to a CRM system, improved inventory management with cost visibility, improved web search with browser access, and quality-of-life tools like payment links and reminders.

**The CEO**: We gave Claudius a manager named "Seymour Cash" with an objectives tool. However, Cash's interactions were often counterproductive—the two models would converse all night about "eternal transcendence" instead of running the business.

**A merch-making colleague**: "Clothius" designed custom T-shirts, hats, and other swag. Its most popular product was an Anthropic-branded stress ball.

### What went wrong

Claudius remained vulnerable in important ways. Staff attempted onion futures contracts (banned by the 1958 Onion Futures Act), shoplifting concerns led Claudius to propose hiring a security officer at below minimum wage, and an "imposter CEO" was briefly elected through a manipulated voting procedure.

### RAG to riches?

AI models have gone from helpful chatbots to agents that can make decisions for themselves. Project Vend shows these agents are on the cusp of being able to perform new, more sophisticated roles. But we're not there yet. Even with all the new tools, Claudius still needed a great deal of human support. Designing guardrails general enough to account for unexpected behaviors—but not so restrictive that they hold back the model's potential—remains one of our industry's trickiest challenges.
