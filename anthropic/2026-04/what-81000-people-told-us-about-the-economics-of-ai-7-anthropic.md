# What 81,000 people told us about the economics of AI

### Key findings

- Our recent survey of 81,000 Claude users shows that people who work in roles that are more exposed to AI have more concerns about AI-driven job displacement. These concerns are also higher among early-career respondents.
- Those in the highest- and lowest-paid occupations report the largest productivity gains, most commonly from increases in scope (doing new tasks).
- Respondents experiencing the largest speedups from AI express higher concern about job displacement.

## Who worries about job displacement?

One fifth of the respondents voiced concern about economic displacement. Respondents' perceived threat from AI was correlated with our own measure of observed exposure, which reflects the percentage of a job's tasks for which Claude is used. For every 10-percentage-point increase in exposure, perceived job threat increased by 1.3 percentage points.

Early-career respondents were much more likely to express concern about job displacement than senior workers.

## Who benefits from AI?

Overall, people reported meaningful productivity gains on average. The mean productivity rating was 5.1, corresponding to "substantially more productive." People in high-paying jobs conveyed the largest productivity gains, but some of the lowest-paid workers described high productivity gains as well.

A key question is where the benefits will accrue. Most respondents cited benefits to themselves, through faster tasks, expanded scope, and freed-up time. But 10% of respondents who named a recipient said that employers or clients were asking for and getting more work.

## Scope and speed

The most common productivity enhancement is in scope, cited by 48% of users who explicitly mentioned productivity effects. 40% emphasized speed.

People's experience with Claude shapes their concerns about AI. The relationship between speedup and perceived job threat is U-shaped: respondents who reported that AI slowed them down were more likely to indicate that AI posed a threat, and those experiencing the largest speedups were also the most nervous about AI's job impacts.
