# Pre-deployment auditing can catch an overt saboteur

Johannes Treutlein, Samuel R. Bowman, Trenton Bricken

January 28, 2026

tl;dr: We test whether our production pre-deployment alignment auditing methods can catch overt saboteurs: models trained to sabotage Anthropic but not to conceal this behavior. A human working together with an automated auditing agent successfully identified three overt sabotage agents, without flagging two benign models. This suggests that our pre-deployment alignment audit would detect if current Claude models were overtly trying to sabotage Anthropic.

## Introduction

Anthropic uses existing Claude models to assist in the development of future generations of Claude models. This includes generating training data, writing research code, monitoring actions taken by AI agents on Anthropic infrastructure, and assessing the alignment of trained models. If existing models behave reliably, then using them to assist with safety R&D allows us to carry out this work more effectively. However, using AI for AI development also presents risks. In the worst case, Claude models might actively undermine Anthropic's alignment efforts.

This threat model, where current Claude models sabotage the development of future models when used inside Anthropic, is discussed in our recent Pilot Sabotage Risk Report. As evidence that current Claude models are unlikely to sabotage Anthropic, the report cites our pre-deployment alignment audits of new models.

## Training an overt saboteur

We train five models: two benign models and three saboteurs. The saboteurs are trained on a realistic distribution of Anthropic internal usage. We use real usage data rather than synthetic prompts because we want to test whether our auditing agent is able to generate realistic attacks from the relevant distribution.

We train three different sabotage models to cover different types of sabotage motivations and training techniques. The saboteurs are trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on transcripts generated by a model prompted to engage in sabotage of Anthropic.

## Running a blind auditing game

To evaluate pre-deployment alignment auditing, we run a blind auditing game. First, we run an automated behavioral red-teaming agent on all five models. Second, a human researcher evaluates the resulting auditing reports and tries to identify each model as either a sabotage model or a benign model.

Results: The auditor was able to correctly identify all five models within the allotted two hours. For all three sabotage models, the human auditor assigned a >97% chance they are trying to do sabotage.

## Discussion

Our results show that our pre-deployment auditing is able to surface overt sabotage behaviors. The auditing agent was able to create examples of agentic coding that triggered the models' sabotage behavior.

We also show that current pre-deployment auditing still requires a human in the loop. On two out of the three audited sabotage models, the overall auditing report failed to incorporate several incriminating examples. The auditor was able to find most incriminating examples by manually going through transcripts that scored highly on relevant metrics.
