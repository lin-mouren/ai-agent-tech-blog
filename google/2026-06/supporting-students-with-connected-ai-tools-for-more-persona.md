Millions of people use Google AI tools to learn new things, both in and out of the classroom. As our AI tools evolve alongside the institutions we serve, here’s a look at new features in [Gemini](http://gemini.google.com/) and [Google Classroom](https://edu.google.com/intl/ALL_us/workspace-for-education/products/classroom/) designed to support students.

## A more effective way to study and learn in Gemini

In the [Gemini app](https://gemini.google.com/), we’re rolling out study notebooks, a dedicated space that turns Gemini into an interactive, adaptive learning platform with personalized lessons for every student based on their learning goal, whether that’s getting ready for a big test or just getting smart on a topic.

Here’s how it works: Students type a prompt or upload class materials and take a diagnostic quiz that identifies the areas they should focus on. From there, Gemini builds a plan of bite-sized, interactive lessons to help them deepen their understanding. As students track their progress with quizzes, their lessons automatically update based on their results and any new material they upload, providing a learning experience that is personalized to them.

Study notebooks also sync with [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com/) so students can seamlessly continue learning there, ask questions about their course materials, and create flashcards, infographics and more. Study notebooks are rolling out now to personal accounts globally and will be available in school-issued accounts in the coming weeks.

## Helping make standardized test prep more accessible

Standardized tests are a huge part of applying to higher education. To help students prepare for such a milestone we’re partnering with experts at [The Princeton Review](https://www.princetonreview.com/) to launch full-length, no-cost practice GRE and ACT tests in Gemini in the coming weeks. We’ll also be adding practice tests for the [Akira ENEM](https://akiraenem.com/) for students in Brazil. After completing a practice test, students will get a detailed breakdown of their performance by topic, so they’ll know exactly what to focus on. Study notebooks also supports the SAT so students can prep with a more structured and personalized feedback loop, and will support more standardized tests soon.

## Responsibly bringing AI to more students in Classroom

We’re also bringing Gemini in Classroom to students of all ages. It’s a safe and trusted way to engage with AI through experiences grounded in their course materials and vetted with pedagogy and safety experts. Through a central destination in Classroom, students can use Gemini to create a study guide, test their knowledge with a quiz or build a deep understanding of topics with [Guided Learning](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/guided-learning/), which is [proven to drive positive learning outcomes](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/measuring-the-impact-of-ai-on-teaching-and-learning/).

Beyond independent study, teachers will soon be able to assign students a study notebook grounded in their specific class materials [directly in Google Classroom](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/iste-2026-educator-updates), where they’ll get insights into where the class or individual student might need extra help so everyone is better prepared for the next exam.



Students of all ages can safely engage with AI through trusted experiences grounded in their course materials and vetted with pedagogy and safety experts directly in Google Classroom.

Learn more about everything we’re announcing in our [launch guide](http://goo.gle/gfe-launch-guide-iste26), and [sign up for updates](https://edu.google.com/intl/ALL_us/communications/) from Google for Education as these features roll out.
